\section{EEPROM Driver}
\label{sec:eeprom}

\paragraph{Group address.}
\begin{equation}
  A = (n_1 + 3n_2) \bmod 256 = (\nOne + 3 \times \nTwo) \bmod 256
    = \ADec = \text{\AHex}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Transaction sequence.}
Each transaction is framed by CS low \ldots\ CS high (\cref{tab:transactions});
opcodes are from~\cite[Tab.~9]{eeprom}. The fitted EEPROM uses a two-byte
address, MSB first~\cite{readme}.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Write and read sequence, in order.}
  \label{tab:transactions}
  \small
  \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l l X r@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Step} & \textbf{Opcode} & \textbf{MOSI $\rightarrow$ MISO} & \textbf{Clocks} \\
    \midrule
    1. WREN  & 0x06 & WREN & 8 \\
    2. WRITE & 0x02 & WRITE, A[15:8], A[7:0], $B$ & 32 \\
    3. RDSR (repeat until ready) & 0x05 & RDSR, dummy $\rightarrow$ status & 16 \\
    4. READ  & 0x03 & READ, A[15:8], A[7:0], dummy $\rightarrow$ data & 32 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Address format.}
After a write with the two-byte address, the status register read
0x00, with WEL~=~0, and address $A$ read back $B$. A write with too
few address bytes is discarded and leaves WEL set~\cite{readme}, so the cleared
WEL bit and the correct read-back confirm the two-byte format.

\paragraph{Write-completion detection.}
The internal write starts when CS rises after the WRITE command. The driver
then polls RDSR until RDY (status bit~0, mask 0x01) reads 0. RDY is 1 while the
write cycle is in progress and 0 when the device is ready~\cite[p.~5,
Tab.~10]{eeprom}. A \SI{50}{\milli\second} timeout guards against a missing
device but is never used as proof of completion.

\paragraph{Result.}
Status before the write: 0x00; after: 0x00.
$B = \text{\BHex}$ was written to $A = \text{\AHex}$, the write completed after
\SI{3}{\milli\second}, and the byte read back as 0x44; verification passed.
